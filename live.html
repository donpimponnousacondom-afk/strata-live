How to read this page

What this is

Strata Live watches Strata (the local model server on port 8080), pi (the coding app) and your hardware, once a second. It never changes Strata. It only reads its status, its logs and pi's session files, and it writes its own logs to the logs folder.

Colours

green = fine · orange = worth a look · red = a problem or near a limit · blue = normal activity · purple = reading a prompt or the SSD.

Getting more detail

Hover over any label, bar, number or column title for a short explanation. Every ? opens the explainer for that panel. Under each event, What this means tells you whether to act and how.

When something goes wrong

  • Look at Errors and events (Problems only).
  • Open the matching Incident recording and replay the minutes before it.
  • Click Make diagnostics bundle to get one zip to share in a bug report.

Words used here

  • Token: a piece of a word, about ¾ of an English word.
  • Context: everything the model sees at once: the whole conversation so far.
  • Compaction: pi summarising old messages to free room in the context.
  • Experts: the model's 24,576 small sub-networks; only a few run for each token.

Normal vs not

  • Writing speed about 30–40 tok/s, GPU at 85 °C or below, more than 400 MiB VRAM free: normal.
  • A timeout at "0 s" that pi retried: harmless.
  • The same request size repeating, or a reply repeating one phrase: a loop; stop it in pi (Esc).

What Strata is doing now

The line of text says the current step. Reading the prompt means taking in the conversation before answering; it is slow when nothing could be reused. Thinking is the model's hidden reasoning. Answering is the visible reply. Writing a tool call means it is asking pi to run something (edit a file, run a command).

Hardware

Live readings, once a second. A bar turns orange above 80% of its scale and red above 92%. The monitor raises a warning when one of these lasts 10 seconds:

  • free VRAM below 250 MiB
  • RAM above 95%
  • GPU at 85 °C or more

On this machine, GPU load near 100% and RAM around 90% are normal while Strata works. It keeps about 40 GB of the model in RAM on purpose. Hover over a bar's name for what it means.

Last requests

Every message pi sends to Strata is one request, newest first. pi sends one per step, so a single answer that edits files and runs commands produces many requests.

Ended:

  • stop / tool_calls: finished normally.
  • length: cut off at the reply limit.
  • disconnect: the app closed the connection, e.g. you pressed Esc.

A high Reused share means Strata kept the conversation from last time and only had to read the new part. 0 reused on a big prompt means a slow full re-read (after compaction or a new chat). Hover over a column title for details.

Writing speed by day

median tokens/s of replies of 100+ tokens

One bar per day and model. The bar is the median writing speed of that day's replies; very short replies are left out because they are noisy. Hover over a bar for the number of replies and how fast prompts were read.

Use it to see whether an engine update or a settings change made things faster or slower. Sampling penalties (presence_penalty) cost a few percent. Heat or another program using the graphics card can cost much more.

A day more than 25% slower than the previous days (with at least 10 replies) is logged as a slowdown. Rebuilt from Strata's own logs the first time the monitor starts.

Relay

The relay is an optional pass-through on port 8090. pi talks to the relay, and the relay forwards to Strata. It records, per request:

  • Answered: how long until Strata replied.
  • First data: when the first words arrived.
  • Longest silence: the longest stretch with nothing sent.

pi gives up after 300 s of silence, so silence turns orange at 60 s and red at 240 s. Requests that never reach the relay never reached Strata either.

Incident recordings

Click Replay to open a recording, then press Play or drag the slider through it second by second. The red line marks when the problem was detected. The white line is the second shown in the table below the chart.

Lines on the chart:

  • blue: writing speed
  • grey: GPU load
  • orange: free graphics memory, from 0 to 1 GB

The strip along the bottom shows what Strata was doing: writing, reading a prompt, idle, or not answering.

Things to look for:

  • Speed dropping while free VRAM hits 0: memory pressure.
  • A long blue stretch with "Written" climbing towards the limit: a runaway or looping reply.
  • Idle right at a pi timeout: the request never reached Strata.

Recordings are saved in logs\incidents.

When something goes wrong (a timeout, an error, low graphics memory, a runaway reply), the monitor saves what Strata and the hardware did from 5 minutes before to 1 minute after. Pick one to replay it second by second.

Errors and events

Everything the monitor noticed, newest first. Sources:

  • strata: the server and its logs
  • pi: pi's session files
  • windows: the system event log
  • config: settings files
  • monitor: Strata Live itself

Levels: error = something failed · warn = worth a look · info = for the record.

Open What this means under any event for an explanation and what, if anything, to do. Hover over a count in the row of totals for the same. Every event is also written to logs\events-<date>.log.
